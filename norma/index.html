Hey Mama Llama,

Just checking in with an update on your plan. I've been thinking about the best way to structure your "homework" to complement the great work you're already doing with your Peloton and other activities.

Instead of just adding more of the same, I'm shifting our focus entirely. We're going to target the specific areas your current system isn't hitting. This is the smart approach to building a truly balanced and resilient body.

Focus 1: Reactivating Your Core

You highlighted your core as a weak spot. As I said on Tuesday, it's not weak at all—it's strong, just de-conditioned. It hasn't been challenged with the intensity we used to. Our first mission is to wake it up and get it firing properly again.

Focus 2: Confident Movement

Mobility isn't just about flexibility; it's about having **confidence** in your movements. Your old injury has created a mental barrier around your left leg, which is completely normal. We saw it in the one-leg balance: when you consciously set up on the left, there was a hesitation that created shakiness and instability.

If we don't actively train this out, that anxiety can grow and create a mental "no-go zone." This can lead to complete avoidance of the issue and, worse, that same anxiety can latch on much easier to any new injuries. Given the number you've acquired recently, if you carry on at this rate we'll have to bubble-wrap you for our sessions! haha

Focus 3: Reclaiming Your Squat

Your Peloton and running have built fantastic leg strength, but they do so in a very specific range of motion. Over time, through a kind of 'gradual de-exposure,' if your knees aren't regularly taken through their full squat range, that natural movement pattern can lessen.

This forces other areas to compensate to get the job done, which is how seemingly unconnected issues can flare up. This can then start the same cycle elsewhere, causing a network of referral issues—all stemming from an issue that wasn't even causing pain in the first place. Our goal is to re-teach this full-range pattern to build a more supple and movement-efficient foundation.

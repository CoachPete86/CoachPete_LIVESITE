Training Designed for the Female Body

Science-based programming that honours women's unique physiology - from menstrual cycle optimisation to pregnancy-safe exercise and menopause support.

Understanding Female Physiology in Training

Why Women Need Different Programming:

  • Hormonal fluctuations affect energy, strength, and recovery
  • Different muscle fibre composition and recovery rates
  • Unique considerations for bone health and joint stability
  • Pregnancy and postpartum physical changes
  • Menopause and its impact on metabolism and bone density
  • Higher risk of certain injuries (ACL, stress fractures)

Menstrual Cycle Periodization

Training with Your Cycle, Not Against It

Research shows that women can optimise their training by aligning workouts with hormonal fluctuations. This approach maximises performance while minimising fatigue and injury risk.

Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5)

Hormones: Low estrogen and progesterone

Energy: Lower, but pain tolerance higher

Training Focus:

  • Gentle movement and mobility
  • Light strength training
  • Yoga and stretching
  • Walking or easy cardio

Follicular Phase (Days 1-13)

Hormones: Rising estrogen

Energy: Gradually increasing

Training Focus:

  • Build training intensity
  • Skill acquisition
  • Higher training volume
  • Strength and power work

Ovulatory Phase (Days 14-16)

Hormones: Peak estrogen, LH surge

Energy: Highest energy and strength

Training Focus:

  • Maximum intensity training
  • Personal record attempts
  • High-intensity intervals
  • Competition or testing days

Luteal Phase (Days 15-28)

Hormones: Rising progesterone

Energy: Decreasing, higher fatigue

Training Focus:

  • Moderate intensity
  • Focus on technique
  • Strength endurance
  • Prepare for deload

Pregnancy and Exercise

Important: Always consult with your GP or midwife before starting or continuing any exercise programme during pregnancy. These guidelines are for educational purposes and should not replace NHS medical advice.

First Trimester (Weeks 1-13)

Key Considerations:

  • Fatigue and nausea may affect energy levels
  • Avoid exercises lying flat on back after 12 weeks
  • Stay hydrated and avoid overheating
  • Listen to your body - pregnancy varies greatly

Safe First Trimester Workout (20 minutes)

Frequency: 3-4 times per week

Warm-up (5 minutes)
  • Gentle marching in place - 1 minute
  • Arm circles and shoulder rolls - 1 minute
  • Pelvic tilts and cat-cow stretches - 1 minute
  • Side bends and gentle twists - 2 minutes
Main Workout (12 minutes)
  • Circuit A (3 rounds):
    • Wall push-ups × 8-12
    • Supported squats × 10-15
    • Modified planks (from knees) × 30 seconds
    • Standing marches × 10 each leg
  • Circuit B (3 rounds):
    • Seated rows (with resistance band) × 10-12
    • Standing calf raises × 15
    • Bird dog holds × 20 seconds each side
    • Breathing squats × 8-10
Cool-down (3 minutes)
  • Prenatal stretches focusing on hips and back
  • Deep breathing exercises
  • Relaxation and mindfulness

Second Trimester (Weeks 14-27)

The "Golden Period" of Pregnancy

Most women feel their best during this time. Energy returns and morning sickness typically subsides.

Exercise Modifications:

  • Avoid supine (lying on back) positions
  • Modify core exercises to prevent diastasis recti
  • Focus on posture and pelvic floor strength
  • Begin incorporating birth preparation exercises

Second Trimester Strength Circuit (25 minutes)

Prenatal Strength Exercises:
  • Goblet Squats - Wide stance for growing belly
    • 3 sets × 10-12 reps
    • Focus on glute activation
  • Incline Push-ups - Hands elevated on bench
    • 3 sets × 8-10 reps
    • Maintains chest and arm strength
  • Single-arm Row - Using dumbbell
    • 3 sets × 10 each arm
    • Supports postural muscles
  • Side-lying Leg Lifts - Hip abductor strength
    • 2 sets × 12 each side
    • Prevents hip pain
  • Modified Planks - Wall or incline position
    • 3 sets × 20-30 seconds
    • Core stability without strain

Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40)

Preparing for Labor and Delivery

Focus shifts to mobility, comfort, and birth preparation while maintaining fitness safely.

Labor Preparation Routine (15-20 minutes daily)

Birth Ball Exercises:
  • Pelvic circles and figure-8s - 3 minutes
  • Gentle bouncing - 2 minutes
  • Supported squats - 10 reps
  • Hip openers and stretches - 5 minutes
Pelvic Floor Focus:
  • Kegel exercises - 3 sets of 10
  • Deep breathing with pelvic awareness
  • Cat-cow stretches - 1 minute
  • Child's pose modifications - 2 minutes

Postpartum Recovery

The Fourth Trimester (0-12 weeks postpartum)

Remember: Get clearance from your healthcare provider before returning to exercise, typically around 6 weeks postpartum (or longer after C-section).

Week 1-2: Gentle Reactivation

Breath and Core Connection
  • Diaphragmatic breathing - 5 minutes, 3x daily
  • Gentle pelvic tilts - 10 reps
  • Transverse abdominis activation - hold 5 seconds × 10
  • Neck and shoulder rolls - 1 minute
  • Walking as tolerated - start with 5-10 minutes

Week 3-6: Progressive Movement

Gentle Reactivation Circuit
  • Wall push-ups - 2 sets × 5-8
  • Glute bridges - 2 sets × 8-10
  • Modified planks - 2 sets × 15 seconds
  • Standing marches - 10 each leg
  • Heel raises - 2 sets × 12
  • Walking - gradually increase to 20-30 minutes

Week 6-12: Gradual Progression

After medical clearance, gradually return to more structured exercise:

  • Start with 50% of pre-pregnancy intensity
  • Progress slowly - increase by 10% each week
  • Pay attention to pelvic floor symptoms
  • Modify exercises for breastfeeding comfort

Menopause and Fitness

Training Through Hormonal Changes

Understanding Menopausal Changes:

  • Declining estrogen affects bone density
  • Metabolic rate decreases
  • Muscle mass naturally declines
  • Sleep quality may be affected
  • Joint stiffness increases
  • Body composition shifts

Exercise Priorities for Menopause:

  1. Strength Training - Combat muscle loss and support bone health
  2. Weight-Bearing Exercise - Maintain bone density
  3. Balance and Coordination - Prevent falls and maintain independence
  4. Flexibility and Mobility - Counter joint stiffness
  5. Cardiovascular Health - Support heart health and mood

Menopause-Optimized Workout (35 minutes)

Frequency: 4 times per week (2 strength, 2 cardio)

Strength Days (25 minutes):
  • Compound Movements (15 minutes):
    • Squats - 3 sets × 8-12
    • Chest press - 3 sets × 8-12
    • Rows - 3 sets × 8-12
    • Overhead press - 3 sets × 8-12
  • Bone-Building Focus (10 minutes):
    • Step-ups - 2 sets × 10 each leg
    • Heel drops - 2 sets × 15
    • Balance challenges - 2 minutes
    • Core stability - 3 minutes
Cardio Days (35 minutes):
  • 5-minute warm-up
  • 20 minutes moderate-intensity cardio
  • 5 minutes high-intensity intervals
  • 5-minute cool-down with stretching

Women-Specific Injury Prevention

ACL Injury Prevention Program

Women are 2-8 times more likely to suffer ACL injuries. This programme reduces risk through neuromuscular training.

ACL Prevention Warm-up (10 minutes)

Perform before all lower body workouts

  • Dynamic Warm-up (3 minutes):
    • Light jogging or marching
    • Leg swings - front/back and side/side
    • High knees and butt kicks
  • Neuromuscular Training (7 minutes):
    • Single-leg balance - 30 seconds each leg
    • Jump landing practice - 10 soft landings
    • Lateral bounds - 8 each direction
    • Glute bridges - 15 reps
    • Clamshells - 12 each side
    • Single-leg squats - 5 each leg

Key Movement Cues for Women:

  • Landing mechanics: Land softly with knees behind toes
  • Knee tracking: Knees stay aligned over toes, don't cave inward
  • Hip activation: Engage glutes before quad-dominant movements
  • Core stability: Maintain neutral spine during all movements

Cycle-Synced Nutrition

Menstrual Phase
  • Increase iron-rich foods (lean meat, spinach, lentils)
  • Anti-inflammatory foods (berries, fatty fish, turmeric)
  • Magnesium for cramp relief (dark chocolate, nuts)
Follicular Phase
  • Light, fresh foods (salads, lean proteins)
  • Complex carbs for energy (quinoa, sweet potatoes)
  • Probiotic foods for gut health
Ovulatory Phase
  • Raw foods and antioxidants
  • Anti-inflammatory spices
  • Fiber-rich foods to support estrogen metabolism
Luteal Phase
  • Complex carbs for serotonin support
  • Healthy fats (avocados, nuts)
  • Magnesium and B6 for PMS symptoms

Body Image and Mental Health

Building a Healthy Relationship with Exercise

Shifting from Punishment to Empowerment

  • Focus on function over form - What can your body do?
  • Celebrate non-scale victories - Strength gains, energy levels
  • Practice self-compassion - Talk to yourself like a good friend
  • Find movement you enjoy - Exercise shouldn't be torture
  • Honor your body's signals - Rest when needed

Mindfulness Practices for Women

Body Appreciation Meditation (5 minutes)
  1. Sit comfortably and close your eyes
  2. Take 5 deep breaths
  3. Starting from your toes, thank each part of your body
  4. Focus on what each part allows you to do
  5. End with gratitude for your body's strength and resilience
